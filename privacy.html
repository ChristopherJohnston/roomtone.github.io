Privacy

Updated 4 November 2026

Room Tone keeps your songs in folders you choose, on your Mac and in your own iCloud Drive or OneDrive. It has no accounts, no servers of its own, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. We don't collect anything about you or your music.

Your library

Each library is a folder you add to Room Tone for Mac. Its songs, recordings, notes, lyrics, ratings and comments stay in that folder. For the iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch, the Mac copies previews and song details into a folder in iCloud Drive or OneDrive, and the apps read them from there. Apple's or Microsoft's privacy terms cover what those services store.

If you share a library's folder with your band, everyone you share it with sees its songs, and the name you've given Room Tone beside your comments, ratings and ideas.

On the iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch

Room Tone asks before using any of these, and works without them.

  • Microphone: to record song ideas. Recordings are saved in your library.
  • Location: to note where you recorded an idea. The place's name and coordinates are kept with the idea in your library. The name comes from Apple's location service, which receives the coordinates to look it up.
  • Speech recognition: to name an idea from what you say at its start. This happens on the device where it can; on devices that can't, Apple's speech recognition service hears the start of the recording.
  • Notifications: made on the device itself, from your libraries. No server sends them.

Siri and Shortcuts actions run on the device. Ideas recorded on Apple Watch go to the iPhone through Apple's connection between them.

On the Mac

Room Tone for Mac includes an optional MCP server, which lets an AI assistant you connect to it, such as Claude, read your libraries. It does nothing until you set it up. What the assistant reads goes to that assistant's provider, under its own terms.

Share links

A link to a song carries the library's and the song's identifiers in the link itself, and can carry the library's name, the song's title, a version and a time. Anyone you send it to can read those. The page a link opens on this site reads them in your browser and sends them nowhere.

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may log the addresses of visitors; see GitHub's privacy statement.

Crash reports

If you choose to share analytics with app developers in your device's settings, Apple sends us reports of crashes, which don't identify you.
